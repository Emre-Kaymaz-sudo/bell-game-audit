# A certificate for the classical value with private input histories

**Result.** For the selected game, a classical team can win at most 22 of the 27 rounds in expectation. It can attain that value by always answering zero. The upper bound allows the players to remember everything they have individually observed. A certificate search also closes the gap between achievable score and upper bound for every game in the original finite grid. [Example certificate](private_certificate.json) · [Grid summary](private_survey.json)

## Model

The referee uniformly shuffles a multiset of labelled cards. Each card specifies a pair of local questions and the XOR required of the two answers. The players know the quotas and target table. They can share randomness independent of the shuffle. During the block, each sees its own questions and own answers. There is no communication or additional information such as scores, joint questions or a copy of the referee's random tape. The draw and subsequent observations do not depend on their answers. The objective is expected total wins.

## A hand-checkable example

The winner's count matrix and target table are

```text
            Questions per block       Target parity
                  Bob                      Bob
Alice 0         4  1  4                  0  0  1
Alice 1         4  4  1                  0  0  0
Alice 2         1  4  4                  1  0  0
```

Take one copy of every pair to form a nine-card rectangle. The remaining 18 cards are three copies of the six high-weight pairs.

* **Rectangle.** Rows of a deterministic local XOR answer table are identical or complements of each other. In the target, the three rows are pairwise distinct and no pair consists of complements. One edit leaves two target rows unchanged, so at least two edits are needed. Answering zero on both sides loses exactly twice.
* **Six-card cycle.** Each local question occurs twice. The XOR of the six predicted parities of a deterministic table is zero, since every local answer appears twice. The XOR of the six required parities is one. At least one prediction fails. Always answering zero attains five wins.

The remaining question is whether a team can improve those two local scores by remembering which cards it has seen. The following conditional simulation rules this out on each component.

## Simulating a private prefix locally

Let a component contain one copy of every pair in its support. Choose a list of pairs of local permutations, including the identity. Require that from **each** possible current card, applying the whole list visits each supported card exactly once.

Fix the position under study and condition on the current card. Put the identity at that position. Use an independent shared random shuffle to assign all other permutations to the remaining positions. Alice applies only the Alice permutation at each position to her current label. Bob does the same using only his own label. The resulting joint card order is exactly uniform conditional on the current card. Each player can therefore simulate its own input history, internal randomness and own past responses, then use its original response rule in an ordinary one-round game. This bounds the private-history win probability at that position by the one-round local value. A fixed optimal one-round response attains it.

For the rectangle, all combinations of a cyclic row shift and a cyclic column shift work. For the six-card cycle, relabel Bob's questions so that the three missing pairs are the diagonal. The six simultaneous permutations of three symbols send any ordered pair of distinct symbols to each of the six ordered unequal pairs exactly once. This is the property the verifier checks. The relabelings reproduce the input distribution. Players receive no score feedback, so the construction also works when a relabeling changes the target parity of a past card.

## Combining the components

Distinguish copies of repeated cards for the proof. A random full shuffle can be generated by uniformly interleaving the component labels and independently shuffling the cards inside each component. To bound the wins on any **one** component, grant the players its positions and the entire order of every other card. This leaves the chosen component's internal order uniform. Its conditional simulation and one-round bound still apply. Sum the component-wise bounds by linearity of expectation. The additional information granted for one bound need not be granted when proving another.

The selected game therefore incurs at least **2 + 3 × 1 = 5 expected losses**. Always answering zero loses exactly the two cells of weights 4 and 1, in every complete block. The two bounds meet. The same value also holds in expectation at each position chosen in advance, since the chance of belonging to a component copy equals its size divided by 27. A particular realized private history can have a different conditional value.

## Reproducible finite-family result

There are 217 integer 3×3 quota matrices in the original search space and 512 target parity matrices. The constructor first attempts the nested supports of each quota matrix. When that decomposition is insufficient, it searches for a packing of uniform rectangles and six-card cycles. Remaining cards can be covered by singletons. The independent verifier checks the action condition on every cell, exact coverage by multiplicity and each component's maximum local score. Any verified packing gives an upper bound. Matching a global deterministic strategy establishes equality.

For the grid survey, flipping all target bits in one row or column amounts to flipping the corresponding local output. There are 16 representative targets with zero first row and first column, and 32 relabelings per representative. The survey verifies **3,472** component certificates with explicit local actions. Using their supports, it also scores **all 64 deterministic response pairs on all 111,104 labelled games**, including the original target bits. In every game the component upper bound equals the achievable static score exactly, using integers. Multiplying every quota by the same positive integer preserves this result, by repeating the certified component cover. No equal-value conclusion is drawn about other quota shapes or intermediate public disclosures.
